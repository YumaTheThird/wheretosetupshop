Problem Statement

Finding the right location can make or break an F&B business.

Singapore's F&B sector is one of the most competitive in Asia. With over 31,000 licensed outlets across 29 planning areas, new entrants face a complex decision landscape spanning rental costs, customer demographics, transport accessibility, and market saturation.

Information is fragmented across multiple sources : URA rental data, NEA licensing records, and census demographics — making it difficult for aspiring operators to form a clear view of any neighbourhood before committing.

Our platform consolidates these signals into a single analytical view, structured around the four factors that most determine location success.

2

3
What will it cost to operate? Rental costs range from $63 to $314/sqm/month. Newer towns often cost more than central ones.
4
Can customers reach you? Areas like Bukit Timah have low public transport density, limiting walk-in traffic despite strong demographics.

🎯 Target Audience

Finding the right location can make or break an F&B business. Our tool helps food vendors analyse demand conditions across 29 planning areas in Singapore, showing where purchasing power is concentrated, who lives there, and what food preferences to expect, so you can choose locations that match your concept and target customer.

This map reflects the proportion of high-income households (earning $10,000/month and above) per planning area, serving as a proxy for consumer spending capacity on F&B.

Purchasing power is heavily concentrated in central and southern planning areas such as Tanglin, River Valley, Downtown Core and Bukit Timah. These areas are well-suited for premium F&B concepts such as fine dining, specialty cafes, and boutique concepts where higher price points are sustainable.

In contrast, areas such as Outram, Bukit Merah and Kallang show higher concentrations of lower-income households. These locations favour affordable, high-volume F&B concepts such as hawker stalls, budget eateries and coffee shops.

Income profiles vary significantly across planning areas, and matching your price point to the local income profile is critical for sustainable F&B operations.

Tanglin, River Valley, Downtown Core and Bukit Timah are dominated by the High ($10K+) tier, indicating residents with strong disposable income and appetite for premium dining experiences.

Outram, Kallang and Geylang show notably larger Low (Below $4K) segments, signalling that affordable concepts with competitive pricing will resonate more strongly with local residents.

Mid-tier areas such as Bishan, Serangoon and Tampines show a more balanced spread across income tiers, making them versatile locations suitable for casual mid-range dining concepts that appeal broadly.

Age composition directly influences dining behaviour. Younger working adults eat out more frequently and spend more per visit, while elderly populations tend to prefer affordable, familiar options.

Downtown Core, Sembawang, Punggol and Woodlands have the highest concentration of working-age residents (20–54). These areas generate strong weekday and weekend dining demand, particularly for lunch sets, casual dining and delivery-friendly concepts.

Outram, Ang Mo Kio, Bukit Merah, Kallang and Toa Payoh show the highest elderly population proportions (18–22%). These areas have strong demand for traditional, affordable food options like hawker fare, zi char, and kopitiam-style concepts perform well here.

Punggol and Sengkang show strong concentration in the 25–39 age bracket, reflecting young families: an underserved segment for family-friendly dining, weekend brunch concepts and casual group dining.

River Valley, Downtown Core, Pasir Ris, Punggol, Sengkang and Choa Chu Kang sit above average on both dimensions, indicating strong purchasing power combined with a large working-age population. These locations offer the most well-rounded demand conditions and can support a wide range of F&B concepts from casual dining to premium experiences.

Downtown Core is the strongest outlier in this quadrant with the highest working-age concentration and among the highest income levels. It is particularly well-suited for lunch concepts, after-work dining and premium casual formats targeting professionals.

Woodlands, Sembawang, Jurong West, Yishun and Bukit Batok have large working-age populations but below-average income levels. These areas generate consistent dining frequency but are price-sensitive. High-volume, affordable concepts with strong value propositions are better positioned here than premium formats.

Tanglin, Bukit Timah, Novena, Bishan and Marine Parade are wealthy but have slightly lower working-age proportions. Demand here is quality-driven rather than frequency-driven. Premium restaurants, specialty cafes and destination dining concepts perform well, though operators should expect lower daily footfall compared to prime locations.

Outram, Ang Mo Kio, Geylang, Toa Payoh, Bukit Merah and Kallang fall below average on both dimensions. These are typically mature estates with older populations and moderate income levels. While established hawker stalls and coffee shops can still thrive here on neighbourhood loyalty and repeat patronage, new entrants face a more challenging demand environment and should enter only with a clear competitive advantage.

Dwelling type is a reliable proxy for household wealth and lifestyle, directly influencing F&B spending behaviour and concept suitability.

River Valley, Downtown Core, Pasir Ris, Punggol, Sengkang and Choa Chu Kang are almost entirely private housing. Residents here are accustomed to higher spending and expect quality dining experiences. Premium restaurants, specialty cafes and concept dining are well-positioned in these areas.

Outram , Woodlands , Jurong West and Jurong East are overwhelmingly public housing areas. These locations have large household volumes but are price-sensitive. High-volume, affordable concepts with strong value propositions perform best.

Novena and Clementi show a meaningful mix of both private and public housing, making them versatile locations where mid-range concepts can attract both segments simultaneously.

Preference for Diversity and High Traffic:

Areas such as Downtown Core and Geylang show strong concentration across multiple F&B types, indicating high diversity. These are central business districts, where mixed-use developments and concentrated commercial activity create environments that support a wide range of food and beverage options (URA, 2025). While these locations can accommodate many concepts, do note competition is also broad and intense.

Preference for Industrial Canteens:

Industrial Estates located in Woodlands, Serangoon, Toa Payoh, Geylang see the most active crowds at Industrial canteens. These locations can be suitable for vendors targeting weekday morning to afternoon demand.

Bukit Merah, Geylang, Bedok, Outram and Kallang show the highest concentration of combined Coffee Shop (CS) and Hawker Centre (HC), indicating strong and proven demand in these areas.

For coffee shops (CS), Bukit Merah leads, followed by Outram and Kallang. For hawker centres (HC), Geylang has the highest stall concentration.

Choosing between Coffee Shop and Hawker Centre

Choose Coffee Shop (CS) if you prioritise stable, repeat customers and lower operational risk. Choose Hawker Centre (HC) if you prioritise high footfall and volume, and can compete in a denser environment.

Choosing between High vs Low Supply Areas

Enter high-supply areas (e.g. Bukit Merah for CS, Geylang for HC) if you want proven demand and are confident in competing. Otherwise, consider lower-supply areas to capture untapped demand with less competition.

More affordable areas include Outram, Geylang, Kallang, and Clementi, as well as central planning areas like Downtown Core — suggesting that older or more fragmented retail stock in these areas may be priced more modestly despite their prime locations.

Users can select up to a few planning areas simultaneously using the filter to compare their rental trajectories. Key patterns to look for:

Steady upward trends suggest a market that is consistently appreciating — entry now may be cheaper than entry in a year's time, but also signals sustained cost pressure.

Irregular spikes (such as the one shown for Pasir Ris above) may indicate data anomalies, short-term demand shocks, or one-off premium transactions — these areas may carry higher rental volatility risk.

Stable or declining trends may present an opportunity if demand conditions in that area remain strong — lower rents in an otherwise viable catchment.

🚇 Accessibility

A location's reach depends on how easily customers can get there. This dashboard maps the Composite Accessibility Score (average MRT exits + bus stops within 500m) at both planning-area and establishment level.

Loading chart…

The choropleth map above provides a nationwide view of public transport accessibility across Singapore's planning areas. Each area is shaded by its Composite Accessibility Score, which is the average number of MRT exits and bus stops within 500m of existing F&B establishments in that area. We chose 500m as the standard for accessibility as that is the number that is the “Golden Standard”. This gives aspiring F&B owners a quick first filter: which areas are generally well-served by public transport, and which are comparatively isolated. Areas with darker shading have higher combined MRT and bus accessibility, making them easier for customers to reach without a car.

Users can select up to a few planning areas simultaneously using the filter to compare their rental trajectories. Key patterns to look for:

High accessibility areas (darker shading) such as Downtown Core and Outram benefit from dense MRT and bus networks — customers can reach these areas conveniently from across Singapore.

Lower accessibility areas (lighter shading) such as Bukit Timah and Tanglin have fewer nearby transport nodes, which may limit walk-in traffic from non-drivers.

Loading chart…

For a more granular perspective, this visualisation allows you to zoom into individual existing establishments and see the specific MRT exits and bus stops within 500m of that specific location. Points are colour-coded by transport type — grey for the selected establishment, blue for nearby bus stops, and yellow for nearby MRT exits. This view is particularly valuable for comparing specific streets or clusters within a planning area, rather than relying on area-wide averages alone. Two establishments in the same planning area may have very different accessibility profiles depending on their exact address.

Ability to use the Licensee Name filter to select a specific establishment and see its surrounding transport infrastructure.

The number of nearby stops shown generally complements the area-level score. However, a high area score but few stops near your specific target address is not entirely impossible and is definitely a red flag worth investigating.

How to use this view: Use the quarter filter to check whether your shortlisted areas remain in the same quadrant over time, or whether they are drifting toward higher costs. A planning area moving rightward across quarters is experiencing rental appreciation — factor this into your longer-term cost projections.

The top-left quadrant represents the most attractive combination: high accessibility and low rental cost. The quadrants can be interpreted as follows:

Top-left (High Accessibility, Low Rent):

The sweet spot for new F&B entrants — good transport connectivity without prohibitive rental costs. Prioritise exploring areas in this quadrant.

Top-right (High Accessibility, High Rent):

Commercially active and well-connected, but expensive. Suitable for operators with strong revenue models or premium concepts that can justify the cost.

Bottom-left (Low Accessibility, Low Rent):

Affordable, but may struggle to attract spontaneous foot traffic. More viable for delivery-first or destination concepts.

Bottom-right (Low Accessibility, High Rent):

The least favourable combination — high cost with limited transport reach. Generally to be avoided unless there are strong specific reasons (e.g. captive audience, anchor tenant proximity).

An important observation from the current data: the relationship between accessibility and rent in Singapore is not straightforward. Newer regional towns such as Punggol, Sengkang, and Jurong West combine relatively high accessibility with high rental costs — driven by modern mall developments rather than centrality. Meanwhile, central areas like Downtown Core and Outram are surprisingly affordable on a per-sqm basis despite strong transport connectivity, potentially offering genuine sweet-spot opportunities. This underscores the importance of using this scatter plot rather than relying on assumptions about which areas are expensive.

Where are the best opportunities?

A neighbourhood may have demand, but still be difficult to enter if the market is already crowded. The scatter plot helps users judge not only whether customers are present, but whether their business can realistically compete here.

Key insights and observations

Central areas, such as Downtown Core, tend to remain commercially intense because the district covers the CBD, Marina Bay and surrounding prime office areas, with a critical mass of office, hotel, retail and complementary mixed uses. That makes it attractive in absolute terms, but also structurally competitive. URA's guidance also shows that the area is being shaped as a denser mixed-use, round-the-clock district rather than a simple neighbourhood catchment, which reinforces the likelihood of intense competition.

This suggests that while Downtown Core remains economically active, the supply intensity is disproportionately high relative to household-driven demand proxies, resulting in a structurally competitive environment. In statistical terms, these areas are characterised by high normalised supply density, high competition clustering, and negative gap values. This reinforces the interpretation that demand in such areas may be driven by non-residential populations (e.g. working population), which are not fully captured by household-based demand metrics in the model.

Some city-fringe and residential planning areas, such as Outram, present more balanced conditions, offering more sustainable entry opportunities. Demand and Supply indices are closer in magnitude, resulting in gap values that are moderately negative. This indicates that supply levels are more proportionate to measured demand, suggesting a more stable competitive environment compared to highly saturated central districts.

However, Outram's relatively lower Demand Index (compared to high-performing residential areas) also explains why it does not emerge as a strong opportunity zone despite its central location.

✨ Stronger Opportunities

Bedok

Bedok is worth attention because it is a large, mature eastern town with established amenities and strong residential catchment. Bedok is attractive for concepts that benefit from recurring local demand rather than one-off destination traffic.

Tampines

Tampines is one of the strongest practical candidates because HDB identifies it as a regional centre, and one of the most developed ones in Singapore. Its town centre includes major malls, office buildings, transport nodes and civic amenities, which means demand is supported by both residents and non-residents. Learn more →

These areas typically exhibit strong underlying demand conditions, lower relative competition, and balanced demographic profiles. The trade-off is that higher activity usually also means higher expectations and stronger competition, so the opportunity is likely best for operators with a clear target segment and differentiated offering.

⚠️ Weaker Opportunities

Downtown Core

Downtown Core should be approached carefully. It is Singapore's global financial district and includes a heavy concentration of prime office space, hotels, retail and mixed-use development. That can create strong demand, but it also raises the bar for entry because competition, rents, and customer expectations are typically much higher. For many small and mid-sized F&B operators, this means the area may be commercially active yet still difficult to enter profitably without a strong niche or differentiated value proposition. URA reference →

Outram

Outram is central and highly connected, but it is also unusual as a catchment. The area is heavily influenced by SGH, Outram Community Hospital, and the broader SGH Campus Masterplan, which make it a major healthcare node rather than a conventional neighbourhood consumption zone. That can benefit certain concepts, especially convenience-led, healthcare-adjacent or worker-oriented formats, but it may be less suitable for operators relying on broad residential lifestyle demand alone.
